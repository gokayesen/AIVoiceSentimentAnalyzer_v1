AI Voice Sentiment Analyzer — Analysis Dashboard: 4 Visual Directions

Same hero screen (the Analysis Dashboard an analyst lands on after opening a completed call) and the same paired session call-list screen, rendered in four distinct visual personalities. Content, data, and IA are held constant across all four — only density, type, motion register, and brand voice change. Every direction shows a low-confidence segment and a text/tone disagreement moment directly in the hero mockup.

1 · Clinical / Data-Dense 2 · Warm / Human-Centered 3 · Editorial / Narrative 4 · Evidence Dossier
Direction 1

Clinical / Data-Dense

Reads like a monitoring or observability console: near-black header chrome, hairline borders, tabular layout, and monospaced numerals for every confidence score and timestamp so figures scan like instrument readouts. Density is high on purpose — an analyst working a queue of calls needs to triage fast, and this register signals precision over warmth. Content areas stay light per the light-mode-only requirement; only the header/sidebar chrome goes near-black, the way many monitoring tools use a dark command strip over a light data surface.

Direction 2

Warm / Human-Centered

Still a professional workplace tool, but softened: warm off-white surfaces, generous rounded corners, breathing-room padding, and a terracotta/sage palette instead of red/green alarm colors. The goal is to make a daily task that involves listening to upset customers feel considerate rather than clinical — pill-shaped timeline segments and rounded cards read as calm and legible rather than alarming, while confidence and conflict states are still shown plainly, just without a harsh visual register.

Direction 3

Editorial / Narrative

Treats the call as a document to be close-read: a serif headline and body typeface, a single reading column for the transcript-plus-timeline, and a narrow margin rail for acoustic evidence presented as annotations — the way a critical edition annotates a text. This leans hardest into the product's real differentiator (reading tone, not just words) by making annotation itself the primary interaction metaphor, rather than burying it in a side panel.

Direction 4 · own choice

Evidence Dossier

This product's actual contract with its user is evidentiary: the model produces exhibits, Elif renders the verdict. A case-file register — kraft-paper surface, exhibit tags, a rubber-stamp treatment for low-confidence and conflicting reads — makes that contract literal rather than implicit in a generic dashboard. It's the direction I'd bet on for this specific product: unlike the clinical console (live-monitoring metaphor), the warm register (approachable but generic), or the editorial register (long-form reading), a dossier metaphor structurally forces every AI output to be framed as evidence to weigh, not an answer to accept — which is exactly the human-in-the-loop posture the PRD and voice guidance both call for.
